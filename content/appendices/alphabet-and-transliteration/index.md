This is the reference table for everything Level 0 teaches and everything the
later levels assume. It gives the alphabet in its dictionary order, the sound
value of each letter, the transliteration this course uses, and — separately —
the handful of contrasts that account for most beginner errors.

## The 33 letters

Sound values are given in IPA. The transliteration column follows the Ukrainian
national system (Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 55 of 2010), which is the
system used on Ukrainian passports, road signs and maps; where a letter is
transliterated differently at the start of a word and inside it, both forms are
given as *initial / elsewhere*.

| # | Letter | Name | IPA | Translit. | Notes |
|--:|:------:|:-----|:----|:----------|:------|
| 1 | А а | а | /ɑ/ | a | Never reduced, even unstressed. |
| 2 | Б б | бе | /b/ | b | |
| 3 | В в | ве | /w/, /ʋ/ | v | Close to English *w* after a vowel: `Львів`. |
| 4 | Г г | ге | /ɦ/ | h | Voiced, breathy — not the German *g*. |
| 5 | Ґ ґ | ґе | /ɡ/ | g | The hard *g*: `ґанок`, `ґудзик`. Rare but real. |
| 6 | Д д | де | /d/ | d | |
| 7 | Е е | е | /ɛ/ | e | As in *bed*. Contrast with `є`. |
| 8 | Є є | є | /jɛ/ | ye / ie | *ye*; softens a preceding consonant. |
| 9 | Ж ж | же | /ʒ/ | zh | As in *measure*. |
| 10 | З з | зе | /z/ | z | The digraph `зг` is written *zgh*. |

The **apostrophe** `'` is not a letter and has no alphabetical position, but it
is not optional either: it blocks the softening that `я є ї ю` would otherwise
carry over, so `м'ясо` is *m-ya-so* and not *mya-so*. It is omitted in
transliteration.

## The four letters that are Ukrainian

`ґ`, `і`, `ї` and `є` are the letters that distinguish the Ukrainian alphabet
from the Russian one. They are also the four most likely to be dropped by a
font, mistyped on the wrong keyboard layout, or read with the wrong value by a
learner who arrived through Russian.

| Contrast | Ukrainian | Sounds like | Common error |
|:---------|:----------|:------------|:-------------|
| `г` vs `ґ` | `гора` vs `ґанок` | /ɦ/ vs /ɡ/ | Reading both as /ɡ/. |
| `і` vs `и` | `сіно` vs `сила` | /i/ vs /ɪ/ | Merging both to /i/. |
| `е` vs `є` | `небо` vs `Європа` | /ɛ/ vs /jɛ/ | Dropping the /j/. |
| `ї` vs `і` | `Україна` vs `Ірина` | /ji/ vs /i/ | Reading `ї` as plain /i/. |

The pair `і`/`и` is the one worth over-practising: it is phonemic, it is
frequent, and it distinguishes real word pairs.

## Reading order: the four blocks

Level 0 does not present the alphabet alphabetically. It presents it in four
blocks, ordered by what a Latin-alphabet reader can safely guess:

1. **Same shape, same sound** — `А Е І К М О Т`. Free.
2. **Same shape, different sound** — `В Н Р С У Х`. The dangerous block:
   `Ресторан` is *restoran*, not *pectopah*.
3. **New shape, familiar sound** — `Б Г Д Ж З И Л П Ф Ц Ч Ш`. Learn by drill.
4. **New shape, new job** — `Ґ Є Ї Й Щ Ь Ю Я` and the apostrophe. The block
   that needs explanation rather than repetition.

## Stress

Ukrainian stress is mobile — it moves between the forms of a single word — and
phonemic: `за́мок` (*castle*) and `замо́к` (*lock*) differ only in where the
stress falls. Ordinary Ukrainian text does not mark it. This course therefore
marks stress with an acute accent in every glossed word and in every vocabulary
list from Level 0 to the end of A1, and drops the marking from A2 onwards,
where stress is learned with the word.

## Transliteration in this course

The national system above is used for all glossed Ukrainian in Level 0 and
early A1, and for personal and place names anywhere in the course. It is chosen
over ISO 9 because it is what learners will actually encounter outside the
course — on a ticket, a signpost or a passport — even though it is lossy
(`г` and `х` both feed English *h*-shaped spellings, and `ь` disappears
entirely). Where a lossless mapping matters, the Cyrillic is given as well; the
transliteration never replaces the Ukrainian, it only accompanies it.

Transliteration support is withdrawn topic by topic through A1 and is absent
from A2 and above.
