## What this is

**Ukrainian as a Foreign Language** is a free, openly licensed Ukrainian course
written by one teacher — S. Le Boulanger — and published in full: fifty-four
units, a model exam beside every unit, level exams, editable slide decks,
printable worksheets, native-voice audio and reference appendices. It is part
of *boulingua*, a family of open language courses that share a layout, a design
system, a five-move unit model and a set of publication standards.

The course runs from a **Level 0 script-onboarding stage** through **CEFR A1,
A2 and B1**. That range is a decision, not an accident: it is the span that can
be built properly by one author with the material and the audio pipeline
available, and it is declared as such. B2 and C1 are stated gaps rather than
quiet omissions.

## Who it is for

Two audiences, taught from the same pages.

**Adults with a reason.** Since 2022 a great many people in Germany and across
Europe have acquired a concrete, non-academic motive for learning Ukrainian:
neighbours, colleagues, family, volunteer work, or a job that suddenly requires
it. That learner is impatient, often self-taught, and usually needs to be able
to read a sign and type a message long before needing the instrumental case.
Level 0 is built for them.

**Secondary pupils.** The German *Gesamtschule* context that the sister courses
serve, where a unit has to survive a forty-five-minute lesson with a mixed
group, an interactive board, and a photocopier. That is why every unit ships an
editable deck and a printable worksheet rather than a link to a platform.

Both are assumed to be starting from zero Cyrillic. Prior knowledge of Russian
is treated as a real and common starting point — helpful for vocabulary,
actively harmful for pronunciation and orthography — and is addressed directly
rather than politely ignored.

## The language taught

Standard literary Ukrainian, following the 2019 «Український правопис». Where
the 2019 norm differs from pre-2019 forms that learners will still meet in
print, the difference is pointed out at the place it matters. Surzhyk and
regional variation are described in intercultural notes, honestly and without
condescension, but they are not taught as models.

The script is Ukrainian Cyrillic in NFC-normalised UTF-8, and the four letters
`ґ і ї є` together with the apostrophe are treated as first-class throughout —
in the prose, in the exam PDFs, in the audio and in the fonts. A course that
renders `ґ` as a box, or reads it aloud as `г`, is teaching Russian with
Ukrainian vocabulary.

## The unit model

Every unit, at every level, follows the same five moves: **Activate → Input →
Practise → Apply → Reflect**. Exam-preparation pages shift to **Task → Model →
Strategy → Attempt → Feedback**. The predictability is the point: a teacher can
plan without reading the whole unit first, and a learner always knows which
part of the page is for checking and which part is for producing.

## Curriculum alignment

Units carry a machine-readable curriculum block: a CEFR level and a set of
can-do statements, each resolving to an identifier in the shared *boulingua
curriculum* registry. A coverage manifest maps units to descriptors, and an
audit script fails the build if a unit claims a descriptor that does not exist.

The declared conformance target is **`core` (A1–B1)**. Under that target, every
in-scope scale that has an A1, A2 or B1 descriptor must be either implemented
in a unit or explicitly recorded as having no official descriptor at that
level. Declaring a gap is a legitimate outcome; the only failure is a scale
that goes missing without being declared. Level 0 maps to pre-A1 descriptors
where they exist and is declared as pedagogical onboarding where they do not.

## What this course is not

- **Not an examination-board product.** The model exams follow the published
  shape of CEFR-level examinations — task types, timings, weightings — but the
  tasks and texts are the author's own. Nothing here is affiliated with, or
  endorsed by, any examination provider.
- **Not a repackaged textbook.** No content is taken from commercial course
  books, in whole, in part, or "inspired by". Reading texts are written for
  this course; where authentic Ukrainian material is used, at B1, it is
  public-domain or openly licensed and is cited in full.
- **Not a political or historical commentary.** Culture and history are part of
  a B1 syllabus and are treated seriously, from open sources, with the sources
  named. They are not a vehicle for anything else.
- **Not finished.** The structure, the level plan and the standards are in
  place; the units land level by level. Each level page states what exists and
  what is planned.
