## What changes at A2

A1 gets by with two cases and one tense. A2 cannot. Two strands run through the
level in parallel:

**The remaining cases.** Genitive, dative, locative and instrumental are
introduced one at a time and always through the function that motivates them —
the genitive through absence and quantity, the dative through the recipient,
the locative through place, the instrumental through means and company. Each
gets a unit of its own and then returns as a secondary focus in later units, so
no case is met once and abandoned.

**Aspect.** Ukrainian verbs come in pairs, and choosing the wrong member of a
pair is the error that most reliably marks a learner. A2 introduces aspect
through the past tense, where the contrast is easiest to feel — *what happened*
against *what was going on* — and treats the two members as vocabulary to be
learned together, never as a rule to be derived.

## Assessment

Sixteen sibling unit exams plus an **A2 level exam** across all four skills,
with duration, total points and grading scale published on the exam page and
the PDF committed under `static/downloads/a2/`.

## Audio from A2 onwards

From A2 every dialogue and every reading text is recorded, not only the
vocabulary. Recordings use a verified Ukrainian voice, checked for `ґ`, `ї`,
`є`, the apostrophe and word stress before any audio ships; transcripts sit
beside the player on the page.
