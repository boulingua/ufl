## 1. Objectives

- *I can recognise all 33 letters of the Ukrainian alphabet in print and give each one a sound value.*
- *I can read aloud short familiar words — names, international words, shop and street signs — without transliteration support.*
- *I can distinguish `і` from `и`, `е` from `є`, and `г` from `ґ` in print, and say why the distinction matters.*

## 2. Input

### 2.1 The alphabet does not have to be learned alphabetically

There are 33 letters. Learning them in dictionary order means spending the
first week on `А Б В Г Ґ Д Е Є` — five of which behave in ways you cannot yet
predict. This unit sorts them instead by **how much you can guess**.

### Block 1 — same shape, same sound (free)

`А  Е  І  К  М  О  Т`

Seven letters you can already read. They look like Latin letters and they do
roughly the same job. That is enough for real words:

| Ukrainian | Transliteration | Meaning |
|:----------|:----------------|:--------|
| ма́ма | mama | mother |
| кіт | kit | cat |
| там | tam | there |
| а́том | atom | atom |
| мак | mak | poppy |
| ко́ма | koma | comma |

### Block 2 — same shape, different sound (dangerous)

`В  Н  Р  С  У  Х`

Six letters that look Latin and are not. This is the block that produces the
classic beginner's misreading — `Ресторан` read letter-shape by letter-shape as
*pectopah*.

| Letter | Looks like | Actually says | Example |
|:------:|:-----------|:--------------|:--------|
| В в | v/B | **v** | ка́ва — *kava*, coffee |
| Н н | H | **n** | сувені́р — *suvenir* |
| Р р | P | **r** (trilled) | метро́ — *metro* |
| С с | c | **s** | курс — *kurs*, course |
| У у | y | **u** | курс — *kurs* |
| Х х | x | **kh**, as in Bach | хліб — *khlib*, bread |

Read these aloud before going on. Cover the transliteration first.

> рестора́н · теа́тр · метро́ · ка́ва · курс · сувені́р

> *restoran · teatr · metro · kava · kurs · suvenir*

### Block 3 — new shape, familiar sound (drill)

`Б  Г  Д  Ж  З  И  Л  П  Ф  Ц  Ч  Ш`

Twelve unfamiliar shapes with sounds you already own. Nothing here needs
explaining — it needs repeating.

| Letter | Sound | Example |
|:------:|:------|:--------|
| Б б | b | хліб — *khlib*, bread |
| Г г | h, breathy | газе́та — *hazeta*, newspaper |
| Д д | d | день — *den*, day |
| Ж ж | zh, as in *measure* | журна́л — *zhurnal*, magazine |
| З з | z | зна́ю — *znaiu*, I know |
| И и | y, between *i* and *e* | лис — *lys*, fox |
| Л л | l | шко́ла — *shkola*, school |
| П п | p | по́шта — *poshta*, post office |
| Ф ф | f | фо́то — *foto*, photograph |
| Ц ц | ts, as in *cats* | цу́кор — *tsukor*, sugar |
| Ч ч | ch | чай — *chai*, tea |
| Ш ш | sh | шко́ла — *shkola*, school |

### Block 4 — new shape, new job (explain)

`Ґ  Є  Ї  Й  Щ  Ь  Ю  Я` and the apostrophe `'`

Eight letters plus one mark that do something no Latin letter does. Units 5 and
6 take them apart properly; for now, recognise them and know what they are for.

| Letter | Says | Example |
|:------:|:-----|:--------|
| Ґ ґ | hard **g** | ґа́нок — *ganok*, porch |
| Є є | **ye** | Євро́па — *Yevropa*, Europe |
| Ї ї | **yi** | Украї́на — *Ukraina*, Ukraine |
| Й й | **y** of *yes* | чай — *chai*, tea |
| Щ щ | **shch** | що — *shcho*, what |
| Ь ь | no sound; softens the letter before it | день — *den*, day |
| Ю ю | **yu** | Ю́рій — *Yurii*, Yuri |
| Я я | **ya** | я — *ya*, I |
| `'` | blocks softening | м'я́со — *miaso*, meat |

### 2.2 The four letters that are Ukrainian

`ґ`, `і`, `ї` and `є` are what separate the Ukrainian alphabet from the Russian
one. If you have read Russian before, this is the part of the unit that is for
you; if you have not, learn these contrasts now and you will never acquire the
habit of ignoring them.

| Pair | Ukrainian | Sounds | Why it matters |
|:-----|:----------|:-------|:---------------|
| `г` / `ґ` | гора́ · ґа́нок | /ɦ/ · /ɡ/ | *hora* is a mountain; *ganok* is a porch. |
| `і` / `и` | ліс · лис | /i/ · /ɪ/ | *lis* is a forest; *lys* is a fox. |
| `е` / `є` | не́бо · Євро́па | /ɛ/ · /jɛ/ | `є` carries a /j/ that `е` does not. |
| `ї` / `і` | Украї́на · Іри́на | /ji/ · /i/ | `ї` is always two sounds. |

### 2.3 Stress

Ukrainian marks stress nowhere in ordinary text, but stress is mobile and it
distinguishes words: `за́мок` is a castle, `замо́к` is a lock. Until the end of
A1 every Ukrainian word in this course carries an acute accent on its stressed
vowel. Read the accent; do not write it.

### 2.4 The people you will keep meeting

Three names, decoded now and used for the next three levels:

> **Окса́на** — *Oksana*. A student in **Льві́в** — *Lviv*.
> **Ма́рко** — *Marko*. Her cousin, abroad.
> **па́ні Ковале́нко** — *pani Kovalenko*. A teacher.

## 3. Practise

### A. Block 1 only

Read aloud, then write the transliteration.

1. `ма́ма` 2. `кіт` 3. `а́том` 4. `там` 5. `ко́ма`

### B. The dangerous block

Read aloud. Do not guess from the shape.

1. `рестора́н` 2. `метро́` 3. `ка́ва` 4. `курс` 5. `сувені́р` 6. `хліб`

### C. Sort the letter

Which block does each letter belong to — 1 (free), 2 (dangerous), 3 (drill) or
4 (new job)?

`Ш` · `О` · `Р` · `Ї` · `Ц` · `У` · `Т` · `Ґ` · `Н` · `Ж`

### D. The four contrasts

Choose the word that matches the meaning.

1. forest — `ліс` / `лис`
2. porch — `гора́` / `ґа́нок`
3. Europe — `Не́бо` / `Євро́па`
4. Ukraine — `Укра́іна` / `Украї́на`

## 4. Apply

**Your own name in Cyrillic.** Write your first name in Ukrainian letters, then
hand it to a partner to read aloud. If what they say is not your name, work out
together which letter did it — it will almost always be one of `и/і`, `е/є`,
`в`, or `г/ґ`.

Some patterns to work from: German *ch* is `х`; German and English *h* is `г`;
a hard *g* is `ґ`; English *w* is `в`; *j* as in *ja/yes* is `й`, or is carried
by `я є ї ю` when it comes before a vowel.

**A six-line sign.** Copy out this list of six shop signs as you would see them
on a street, then read the whole list aloud without looking at any
transliteration:

> `ПО́ШТА` · `БАНК` · `КА́ВА` · `ТЕА́ТР` · `МЕТРО́` · `ШКО́ЛА`

## 5. Reflect

- [ ] I can name all four blocks and say what makes each one different.
- [ ] I can read `Ресторан` correctly and explain why *pectopah* is wrong.
- [ ] I can point to `ґ`, `і`, `ї` and `є` and say what each one does.
- [ ] I know that stress is not written in real Ukrainian text.

**One line in your notebook:** write the three names of the cast — `Окса́на`,
`Ма́рко`, `Ковале́нко` — from memory, in Cyrillic.

## Common pitfalls

- Reading `В` as *b*. It is *v*: `ка́ва` is *kava*, not *kaba*.
- Reading `Г` as a German *g*. It is a breathy /ɦ/; the hard *g* is `ґ`, which
  is a separate letter with its own place in the alphabet.
- Treating `и` as an *n* because of its shape, or as the same vowel as `і`. It
  is a distinct vowel and it is never word-initial.
- Reading `Ї` as `І`. `Ї` is always two sounds — *yi* — and it is one of the
  letters that identifies the text as Ukrainian.
- Writing the stress accents into your own Ukrainian. They belong to
  dictionaries and to teaching material, not to ordinary writing.
