## Why a level before A1

Ukrainian is written in a 33-letter Cyrillic alphabet. A learner arriving from
German, English or French cannot read the target language on day one, which
means every ordinary beginner activity — matching a word to a picture, reading
a name, copying a phrase — silently assumes a skill that has not been taught.
Level 0 teaches it explicitly and then gets out of the way.

Three things make this harder than a simple letter-for-letter swap, and each
one gets its own unit:

1. **False friends of the eye.** `В Н Р С У Х` are all familiar shapes with
   unfamiliar values. Learners who guess from Latin read `Ресторан` as
   *Pectopah*, and the habit is hard to break later.
2. **The Ukrainian letters.** `ґ  і  ї  є` distinguish Ukrainian from Russian
   Cyrillic. Learners who arrive via Russian — a large share of this course's
   audience — routinely collapse `і/и`, `е/є` and `г/ґ`. These contrasts are
   drilled from Unit 1 rather than treated as a footnote.
3. **Stress.** Ukrainian stress is mobile and phonemic, and it is not written
   in ordinary text. Every glossed word in this course carries a stress mark
   until the end of A1.

## Transliteration policy

Every glossed Ukrainian token in Level 0 and early A1 carries a Latin
transliteration and a short pronunciation hint. The transliteration follows the
Ukrainian national system (as used for passports and place names), so that what
learners read here matches what they will see on maps, tickets and signs. It is
a crutch with a scheduled end: transliteration is withdrawn topic by topic
through A1 and does not appear in A2 or above.

{{< callout type="warning" title="Cursive is optional" >}}
Ukrainian cursive differs markedly from print — handwritten `и`, `п`, `т` and
`ш` are a genuine second alphabet. Unit 6 teaches enough cursive to *read* a
handwritten note, and offers a full handwriting worksheet track for those who
want it. Cursive production is **not** required to progress to A1.
{{< /callout >}}

## Assessment

Level 0 has one sibling exam per unit and a **Level 0 exam** that is a decoding
test, not a language test: read aloud, transcribe from dictation, and type a
short list of words containing `ґ`, `ї`, `є`, `ь` and the apostrophe. It is
passed on accuracy of decoding and keyboard production, and it is the gate into
A1.

## Curriculum mapping

Level 0 is pedagogical onboarding. It maps to pre-A1 descriptors where the
Companion Volume provides them, and where it does not, the gap is declared
explicitly rather than papered over. The machine-readable scope declaration for
the whole course lives in `conformance.yml` in the repository.
